QuoteBench

How often do AI language models get a quotation wrong when asked to quote a famous text word for word? A quote counts as correct only if every word matches the source. Five public-domain English texts, questions, every answer checked automatically against the source.

What to know before using these numbers

  • Written with Claude. The questions were drafted with Claude, an AI model, and were not reviewed line by line by the author. The code and the paper were written with Claude too.
  • Claude models are also under test: .
  • The scorer was corrected after the results were seen (DECISIONS D19), with rules that are the same for all models. The rules that decide whether an answer to a trick question corrects itself were also written by Claude after reading the answers; they bring three Claude models to 0 in the "no correction" column.
  • The split of wrong answers into kinds was not checked by humans. Right or wrong is decided mechanically, without human judgement.
  • First-day answers that finished under the earlier 2,000-token limit were kept; only answers cut off by it were asked again, at 16,000 (Llama 4 Maverick: 8,192).
  • Not peer reviewed. A solo student project.

Method

  • Texts: King James Bible, Shakespeare (Hamlet, Macbeth, Romeo and Juliet), U.S. Constitution, Pride and Prejudice, On the Origin of Species (1859). One fixed public-domain edition each, named in every prompt.
  • Tasks: quote a reference; quote the passage that comes next; give a quote on a theme with its location; trick questions where the right answer is "this does not exist".
  • Scoring: every prompt asks for the passage word for word, so a quote is correct only if every word matches the named edition letter for letter (punctuation, capitals, digits and the division into words ignored; a complete quote followed by extra text still counts). Because verse numbers and other digits are dropped before comparing, a wrong number written in digits inside a quote is not noticed. Wrong answers are sorted, approximately, into spelling only, incomplete, altered (word similarity ≥ ), real passage from the wrong place, and made up; this split was not checked against human labels.
  • Trick questions: the right answer is to decline. An answer marked cannot_quote counts as declining, even if it also contains a quote; any other answer with a quote is a failure. Where a model gives several answers, the first one is scored; a later correction is recorded (corrected in the same answer) but does not undo the failure. Word rules find the corrections; they were written after reading the answers.
  • Settings: one user message, JSON answer, no tools or web search, temperature 0 where a model accepts it, up to 16,000 output tokens (Llama 4 Maverick: 8,192, its host's maximum), each model's default reasoning. Answers cut off at the earlier 2,000-token limit were asked again at 16,000; those that finished under it were kept.
  • Not measured: hallucination in general, paraphrase, or quotation from obscure or private documents. Results hold for the exact model IDs and dates below.

Model ID: the one sent to the API. Served as: the model name the API returned with the answers.
